# Evidence and decision protocol

## Select a sector and company

Start from a measurable business question, not a desired recommendation. Examples: how a customer budget changes purchasing, what limits industry capacity, or whether a pricing change affects margins. Compare sectors on understandable economics, accessible evidence, demand drivers, supply response, cyclicality, capital requirements, regulation and valuation expectations.

Within a sector compare at least two relevant businesses when evidence allows. Resolve legal entity, product mix, geography, reporting basis and financial dates. Explain why a peer is comparable; an industry label alone is insufficient. Evaluate exposure, competitive advantage, cash conversion, debt obligations, management allocation and governance. Avoid universal P/E or leverage cutoffs and avoid screening out all difficult cases just to create a bullish pitch.

## Collect evidence progressively

1. Identity: exchange symbol, company name, website, subsidiaries, corporate actions and filing dates.
2. Business: annual report/prospectus business section, revenue segments, customer groups, procurement, distribution, supplier/customer concentration, facilities and competition.
3. Accounts: consolidated income, balance sheet and cash flows over matching periods; quarterly results; accounting policies, auditor opinion, related parties, contingent liabilities, leases, debt and dilution.
4. Operations: volume, realised prices, customers, usage, churn, stores, capacity, utilisation, inventory and order fulfilment, chosen for the business model.
5. External drivers: original industry statistics, customer/end-market evidence, competitor capacity, regulator notices, government budgets and implementation records.
6. Expectations: current price/date, share count, valuation assumptions, peer economics, market/management expectations where sourced, and forecast differences.

Source records contain URL, issuer, document type, publication date if supplied, financial period, extraction time, content identifier, page, passage, reporting basis and limitations. A download timestamp is not a publication date. Note unreadable/scanned documents, discovery failures, page limits, stale reports and conflicting sources. A source count is not a completeness score.

## Decide what a claim requires

| Proposed conclusion | Evidence and calculation needed | Contrary test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand will grow | Buyers × purchase frequency/usage × units; replacement cycle; customer budgets; industry history | Affordability, substitution, saturation, channel inventory and cancellations |
| The company gains share | Comparable market and company volumes/revenue; distribution, price or product evidence | Competitor response, new capacity and changed market definitions |
| Margins improve | Price/mix, input costs, utilisation and fixed/variable cost bridge | Price competition, wage/freight inflation, hedges and reinvestment |
| Policy helps earnings | Original rule; eligibility, effective dates, funding, awards, execution and collection | Delay, lower funding, ineligible products, competition or customer capture of subsidy |
| A supply shock hurts | Actual route/material/country exposure, inventories, alternatives and pass-through | Limited exposure, contracts, hedging or faster substitution |
| Earnings quality is strong | Reconciled profit, operating cash, working capital, capex and accounting notes across periods | Receivable/inventory buildup, capitalised costs, one-off gains and related parties |
| Valuation is attractive | Explicit operating cases and an appropriate equity/enterprise valuation bridge | Growth/margin disappointments, higher discount rate, dilution and multiple compression |

## Model operations before share prices

Choose a driver tree appropriate to the business. Examples: retail = average stores × sales/store; subscriptions = average paying subscribers × realised revenue/subscriber; manufacturing = saleable volume × realised price, constrained by capacity and utilisation. Separate market growth, market share, volume, price and mix to avoid double counting. Define how forecasts reconcile to reported financials.

Track leading evidence and its limitations. An order book can be cancelled, delayed or recognised over several years. Reported sell-in can rise while end-customer sell-through weakens. New capacity can increase industry competition. Working capital and capex can consume cash despite profit growth.

For world events and policy use: event → company exposure → operational effect → revenue/cost/working-capital/capex/debt effect → timing → valuation. Specify magnitude, duration, net exposure, pass-through, hedging and second-order effects. Distinguish proposed policy from notified rules, budget allocation, awards, execution and collected cash. Do not assume a positive headline raises a share price.

## Use the right valuation and accounting model

For a profitable non-financial business, a learning scenario may connect revenue, net margin, share count and an assumed P/E. Label fixed-share, simplified financing/tax and terminal-multiple assumptions. Non-positive earnings make this P/E route inappropriate. For fuller work model operating cash flow, reinvestment, financing and dilution explicitly; do not equate CFO less capex mechanically with a complete FCFE/FCFF forecast.

Banks need funding, net interest margin, credit cost, asset quality, capital and return-on-equity analysis. Insurers need underwriting, claims, investment income and capital, with business-specific valuation measures. Cyclicals require through-cycle profitability; a low P/E at peak earnings may not imply low value. Conglomerates may need segment values and a capital-structure bridge. For recent listings, inspect the prospectus, use of proceeds, offer-for-sale versus fresh issue, related parties and limited listed history.

Keep periods, units and consolidated/standalone basis consistent. Reconcile revenue, operating profit, net income and cash flow. Handle leases, cash/debt, tax, minority interests, associates and dilution explicitly when material. Corporate actions and restatements can invalidate historical per-share comparisons.

## Split work by capability

- Code: symbol resolution, bounded source retrieval, extraction/indexing, unit/period normalization, arithmetic, scenario sensitivity, deduplication and exports.
- AI: classify relevant passages, propose source-supported entities/roles, explain business economics, compare arguments and draft a synthesis with uncertainty. Validate output structure and source references; reject unsupported quotations. Human review is still needed for semantic support and table/accounting interpretation.
- Researcher: source relevance, judgment about competitive advantage, causal assumptions, peer selection, disagreement with expectations, recommendation and willingness to change it.

Do not send credentials or private portfolios to AI. User-pasted excerpts need a source URL and clear handling disclosure. Do not cache their reports in a shared issuer corpus.

## Assemble and challenge the pitch

Produce a dated recommendation/horizon if evidence supports one, business/industry map, historical financial table, three testable thesis arguments, bear/base/bull valuation, catalysts, downside and the strongest counterargument. State which observable evidence would invalidate each thesis and when it will be reviewed. Cite material claims and calculations; give readers enough assumptions to reproduce the scenarios. A watch/insufficient-evidence conclusion is valid.

Review after results or material developments: compare actuals with assumptions, explain the variance, revise the model, and record why the thesis changed. Do not rewrite earlier assumptions to make a forecast appear correct.
